# Sales Proposal Playbook

Reusable, vertical-agnostic plan for pitching and closing any local business. Follows the sales motion in CLAUDE.md (demo → propose → trial → close → onboard).

> **Reality check:** the full motion (especially the trial website) requires the platform through Phase 3 (website generator). Until then, the first 1–2 clients are **pilot clients** — see "Pilot-client variant" at the bottom.

---

## Stage 1 — The pitch meeting (30–45 min)

**Goal:** not to close — to get them to agree to a free trial site.

1. **Open with their pain, not your product** (5 min). Ask:
   - "How do new customers usually find and contact you?"
   - "What happens when someone WhatsApps you at 11pm?"
   - "How do you keep track of a customer from first message to job done?"
   - "When's the last time a lead just… disappeared?"
   Write their answers down — they become the proposal's opening lines.
2. **Demo the MegaStar Arena CRM live** (10 min). Frame: "This runs my venue every day — real staff, real events. I'm not selling you software I read about; I'm selling you the system I run my own business on." Show: pipeline board, a contact's full history, the calendar.
3. **Bridge to them** (5 min): "Now imagine this, but built around *your* workflow — your stages, your services, your customers." Sketch their pipeline on paper using the steps they described in step 1. This moment — seeing THEIR process as a pipeline — is the hook.
4. **The offer** (5 min): "I'll build you a working version — your name, your services, your website — free, no commitment. You test it for 2 weeks. If it doesn't make your life easier, we shake hands and part ways."
5. **Before leaving:** book the intake conversation (§1–5 of the intake checklist) on the spot. A pitch that ends with "I'll call you" is dead.

## Stage 2 — Build the trial (target: within 1 week of intake)

- Build the trial site + CRM from intake §1–4: their branding, real services, real prices, and a working online-booking flow.
- Preload 3–5 of their real customers into the pipeline (from their WhatsApp/notes) so the CRM demo isn't empty.
- Send them the link with zero ceremony: "It's live. Try booking yourself in like a customer would."
- **Seeing their own business live — their services bookable online, their real customers already sitting in the pipeline — is the wow moment.** Make sure the booking flow works end-to-end before sending.

## Stage 3 — The close (during trial week 2)

Present a one-page quote:

| | |
|---|---|
| System setup, website, CRM configuration, data import | **FREE** (worth RM 5,000+ if commissioned separately) |
| Monthly subscription — Managed | **RM 1,200/month** |
| Contract | 12 months |
| Everything included | Website + hosting, booking system, CRM, review automation, follow-up sequences, unlimited maintenance & content updates by Jacky |
| After 12 months | Renew Managed, **or** switch to Self-Serve at **RM 500/month** (same system, same data, you drive) |

**Framing lines that work:**
- Anchor against alternatives: "A basic website alone costs RM 3–8k from an agency, then RM 100+/month hosting, then a booking tool at RM 200/month — and none of it talks to each other. This is all of it, one price, and I maintain it."
- Anchor against headcount: "RM 1,200/month is a fraction of one admin staff salary — and this system takes bookings at 3am and never forgets a follow-up."
- The lock-in is fair because setup is free: "I invest weeks of setup at no charge; the 12 months is how I earn that back. That's why there's no setup fee."

**Objection handling:**
- *"Too expensive"* → don't discount the price; shrink the scope is not an option either (it's productized). Re-anchor on the admin-staff comparison and what a lost lead costs them per month.
- *"What if you disappear / get too busy?"* → "Your data is yours. After the first year you can run it self-serve without me. And I cap how many managed clients I take so every client actually gets my attention." (True — cap is 3–5.)
- *"We already handle everything on WhatsApp"* → "Keep WhatsApp. This catches the customers who *don't* WhatsApp you — the ones googling at night who'd rather book online than message a stranger. It adds a front door; it doesn't replace yours."
- *"1 year is long"* → "That's what makes the RM 5k setup free. Month-to-month with a setup fee is the alternative — nobody prefers it once they do the math."
- *"Let me think about it"* → set a real deadline: "The trial site stays live until [date]. After that I take it down and the slot goes to the next business on my list." (Also true — Managed slots are genuinely capped.)

## Stage 4 — Sign & onboard

1. Sign agreement (lock-in, PDPA acknowledgment — see CLAUDE.md governance flags)
2. First month's payment = project start
3. Complete remaining intake (§6–9): booking rules, FAQ/tone, domain/GBP access, customer-list import
4. Go-live target: **within 2–3 weeks of signing** (setup work happens within the 1–2 day/week budget — don't promise faster)
5. Handover call: 30-min walkthrough with their staff, create their logins

## Ongoing (Managed year)

- All maintenance/updates via a single channel (WhatsApp to Jacky) — small changes batched weekly, not on-demand
- Month 11: renewal conversation — renew Managed at RM 1,200 or move to Self-Serve at RM 500 (frame as their choice, no pressure; a self-serve client at RM 500 with zero hours from you is still a good client)

---

## Pilot-client variant (first 1–2 clients only, while the platform is still being built)

- Be honest: "You're my founding client — you get the system shaped around you."
- Offer: **50% off year one (RM 600/month)** in exchange for (a) patience while features land, (b) a testimonial + permission to demo their system to future prospects (anonymized data), (c) weekly feedback.
- Everything else (free setup, lock-in, Managed model) stays the same — the discount is the pilot compensation, don't stack more concessions on top.

## Rules for Jacky (don't break these under sales pressure)
- Never waive the lock-in — free setup + no lock-in = you carry all the risk.
- Never oversell current Managed capacity — scarcity claims must always be literally true. Today that's 3–5 clients (part-time, solo); the number grows when delivery capacity does, and the pitch language updates with it.
- Never promise a feature that isn't built. Demo only what exists; roadmap talk creates roadmap debt.
- Price is the price. If they can't afford RM 1,200, they're not the customer — a discount client costs the same hours as a full-price one.
